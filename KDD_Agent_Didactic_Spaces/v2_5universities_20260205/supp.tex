\section{What is in this supplement}
This supplement gathers material that exceeds the 7-page main paper. It includes:
(i) details of stakeholder engagement (formative interviews) and the practitioner validation pilot (demographics, full rating distributions, and extended quotes);
(ii) complete empirical tables and figures from the offline runs on \texttt{unified\_v3};
(iii) an expanded mission-corpus check (56 missions);
(iv) an O*NET \emph{time-travel} audit that measures market drift across historical releases (2022 - 2025);
(v) qualitative edge cases; and
(vi) additional method details (lenses, alternative constraints, pathway aggregation, and robustness protocols).

\paragraph{Protocol.} Semi-structured interviews presented participants with Pareto-front visualizations generated by \ADS/\MAPE for representative course-design scenarios (e.g., market vs. pedagogy/mission trade-offs). Questions probed: (i) problem realism; (ii) interpretability and usefulness vs. ranked lists; (iii) which option they would pick and why; (iv) who would use the tool; (v) missing information (uncertainty, constraints, recourse); and (vi) perceived social impact.

\paragraph{Consent and anonymization.} At the start of each session, the interviewer stated that the interview would be recorded for research purposes and that any quotes used in publications would be anonymized; participants agreed.

\paragraph{Themes (representative anonymized excerpts).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item {Problem realism (E1):} ``That tension between market demand and learning quality is basically my daily reality.''
  \item {Decision politics (E1):} ``Whoever has the louder voice that day wins.''
  \item {Pareto as a decision map (E3):} ``This isn't a ranking it's a decision map.''
  \item {Governance guardrail (E2):} ``A guardrail against market dominance is basically governance for educational integrity.''
  \item {Social impact (E1):} ``It's a democratization play.''
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Practitioner-suggested improvements.} Recurring requests included: clearer control knobs (design moves that shift a solution along the frontier), interactive drill-down into individual Pareto points (retrieved evidence and uncertainty cues), uncertainty/freshness indicators for market targets, operational constraints (cost/time/capacity), strict evidence/provenance modes, and richer what-if / recourse tools.

\subsection{Practitioner validation pilot ($n{=}13$)}

\paragraph{Notes on boundary cases.} One participant (P06, professor emeritus) expressed skepticism about \emph{reification} and \emph{oversimplification} when interpreting embedding similarity as ground truth. At the same time, they acknowledged value ``as a heuristic for structuring discussion.'' We treat this as a boundary condition: \ADS/\MAPE is a governance scaffold, not a substitute for expert judgement, and should be paired with provenance, uncertainty cues, and human oversight.

\subsection{Planned next study: controlled A/B comparison (future work)}
We outline a minimal next-step study focused on within-subject comparison of (A) ranked-list scalarization vs. (B) Pareto set + dominance guardrail. This will test whether Pareto-based trade-off presentation improves interpretability, contestability, and defensibility of curriculum decisions under realistic time constraints.

\paragraph{Participants.} 5 - 10 practitioners involved in course design (instructional designers, course creators, and/or program leads). Recruitment will be via the partner network; participation will be voluntary.

\paragraph{Stimuli.} 3 - 4 course-design scenarios. For each scenario we generate: (i) a Pareto set with 5 - 8 highlighted feasible options plus retrieval-grounded explanations and recourse cues, and (ii) a baseline ranked list produced by weighted-sum scalarization using the same objectives.

\paragraph{Conditions.} Within-subject comparison between:
(A) {Ranked list (scalarization)} and
(B) {Pareto set + dominance guardrail} (\MAPE with the default $\tau$).
Order will be counterbalanced across participants and scenarios.

\paragraph{Outcome measures.} We will collect:
(i) selection time; (ii) self-reported confidence and perceived autonomy; (iii) perceived usefulness/interpretability/actionability; (iv) ability to articulate trade-offs (coded from short written justifications); and (v) qualitative feedback on missing signals (uncertainty, constraints, evidence).

\paragraph{Procedure.} For each scenario, participants review the output, choose an option, and provide a brief rationale. Semi-structured follow-up questions probe interpretability, governance value (e.g., setting/justifying $\tau$), and desired ``control knobs'' (design moves that shift a solution along objectives).

\paragraph{Ethics and reporting.} Participation will be based on informed consent; quotations will be anonymized; no sensitive personal data will be collected. Results will be reported as descriptive statistics plus a thematic synthesis.

\paragraph{Interview guide (question prompts).} The semi-structured guide used in the interviews and pilot includes prompts on: first reaction; comparison to current practice; interpreting trade-offs; choosing and justifying an option; missing information (uncertainty, constraints, recourse); who would use the tool; governance guardrails (dominance threshold); social impact; and suggested improvements.

\section{O*NET time travel: market drift audit}
\label{sec:supp-time-travel}
One of the core design claims of \ADS is that \emph{stakeholder targets are explicit, inspectable inputs}\, and therefore can be \emph{updated} when the external world changes. In practice, labour-market signals drift over time: occupation profiles, hot technologies, and emerging tasks evolve faster than university curricula. To test whether \ADS/\MAPE makes such drift visible (rather than silently baking it into a single score), we rerun {ADS-2D (market+mission)} while holding the course corpus fixed and swapping the market target corpus to different historical O*NET releases.

We use four releases spanning three years: v26.3 (2022-05), v27.3 (2023-05), v28.1 (2023-11), and v30.0 (2025-08). Each release contains \NumOccupations occupations (same definitions), but differs in associated signals (e.g., hot technologies and emerging tasks). We run 3 seeds (0,1,2) with MiniLM SBERT and $\tau{=}0.25$.

\section{Lens variants}
\subsection{Identity and diagonal lenses}
In the prototype, a lens $L_a:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}^d$ is a linear reweighting of embedding dimensions.
The identity lens is $L_a(v)=v$.
A diagonal lens is $L_a(v)=\mathrm{diag}(\ell_a)\,v$, where $\ell_a\in\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^d$.
Diagonal lenses are easy to audit and can be interpreted as feature importances over embedding dimensions.

\subsection{Learned diagonal lenses (prototype)}
The learned diagonal lens emphasizes dimensions predictive of whether a text belongs to a stakeholder corpus.
A lightweight implementation trains a one-vs-rest logistic regression classifier on embeddings to separate stakeholder target texts from background texts.
Let $w_a\in\mathbb{R}^d$ denote the learned weight vector. We form a nonnegative diagonal weight vector by $\ell_a = |w_a|$ (or a clipped/normalized variant), and set $L_a(v)=\mathrm{diag}(\ell_a)\,v$.
{Note:} this is an interpretable lens; richer lens learning (e.g., low-rank or neural transforms) is possible but not required for the ADS formalism.

\section{Pathway (sequence) scoring variants}
The main paper focuses on option-level evaluation ($T=1$). For multi-step pathways $\pi=(x_1,\dots,x_T)$, the prototype supports several aggregation choices:
\begin{itemize}
  \item {Profile aggregation:} form $v_\pi = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T v_{x_t}$ and compute $s_a(\pi)=\cos(L_a(v_\pi),L_a(\mu_a))$.
  \item {Score aggregation:} compute per-step scores $s_a(x_t)$ and aggregate $s_a(\pi)=\sum_t w_t s_a(x_t)$ with optional discounting.
  \item {Constraint-aware aggregation:} require feasibility at each step (or constrain cumulative dominance gap).
\end{itemize}
These variants separate representation effects (embedding + lenses) from planning effects (search under prerequisites).

\section{Dominance / autonomy-drift constraints}
The main paper uses a dominance-gap constraint $g(x)=s_{\text{market}}(x)-\max_{b\ne \text{market}} s_b(x)$.
Alternative constraints that can be substituted without changing the rest of the pipeline include:
\begin{itemize}
  \item {Max-gap across all stakeholders:} $g(x)=\max_{a,b}(s_a(x)-s_b(x))$.
  \item {Market-vs-others average:} $g(x)=s_{\text{market}}(x)-\frac{1}{m-1}\sum_{a\ne \text{market}} s_a(x)$.
  \item {Weighted dominance:} $g(x)=s_{\text{market}}(x)-\sum_{a\ne \text{market}} \lambda_a s_a(x)$ with governance weights $\lambda_a$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Stability metrics and perturbations}
\subsection{Set overlap}
Given two Pareto sets $\mathcal{P}$ and $\mathcal{P}'$, we measure overlap with the Jaccard index
\begin{equation}
  J(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}') = \frac{|\mathcal{P}\cap\mathcal{P}'|}{|\mathcal{P}\cup\mathcal{P}'|}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Perturbation procedures (prototype)}
We apply three perturbation families:
(i) {embedding/score noise:} add small isotropic noise before normalization;
(ii) {target perturbation:} resample a fraction of the target corpus and recompute $\mu_a$;
(iii) {lens noise:} perturb diagonal weights $\ell_a$ by multiplicative noise.

\section{Dataset provenance and strict mode}
\subsection{Provenance manifests and hashing}
To support auditability and reproducibility, we maintain a provenance manifest for each dataset snapshot.
For every retrieved artefact we record at minimum:
(i) a stable identifier, (ii) source URL, (iii) snapshot timestamp, (iv) a content hash of the raw text, and (v) parsing/extraction rules.

\subsection{Strict validation checks}
All experiments are run in a strict mode that validates:
(i) schema completeness (required fields present),
(ii) minimum text length / non-empty artefacts,
(iii) deduplication of near-identical artefacts,
and (iv) basic PII screening to exclude names/emails/phone numbers.
