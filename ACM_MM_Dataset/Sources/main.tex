%% Abstract
\begin{abstract}
Course text descriptions implicitly encode multimedia instructional design: a sentence-embedding model predicts whether a course includes video lectures ($F_1{=}{\SBERTVideoF}$), and the primary axis of a cross-national educational embedding space correlates with multimedia resource richness ($r{=}0.41$).
We demonstrate this with \ADSMM, a multimodal educational resource dataset spanning {\NumCourses} courses from {\NumUniversities} universities across {\NumCountries} countries (USA, Sweden, UK, Russia), augmented with structured multimedia annotations from MIT OpenCourseWare ({\NumResourceTypes} resource types, {\NumOCWVideo} with video, {\NumImages} cover images) and instructional activity metadata from MISIS ({\NumMISIS} courses with hours breakdowns and competency codes).
Latent space analysis on 36{,}192 records reveals a \emph{unified didactic space}: courses from all institutions overlap in embedding space (separation ratio 0.70) and 36\% of nearest neighbors cross institutional boundaries.
We define cross-modal evaluation tasks with baselines (Spearman $\rho{=}{\SBERTRichnessRho}$ for richness prediction, macro $F_1{=}{\SBERTMacroF}$ for resource type classification) and release all data, images, code, and baselines.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Educational resources are inherently multimodal: a university course may combine textual descriptions, video lectures, interactive simulations, problem sets, and visual materials into a coherent learning experience~\cite{mayer2009multimedia,wang2024llmedu}.
Yet existing educational datasets and benchmarks overwhelmingly treat courses as text-only artifacts---a course title and description, perhaps with metadata about prerequisites and credit hours~\cite{morsy2019course2vec,yu2023mooccubex,dasilva2022slrERS}.
This text-only view obscures a fundamental question: \emph{how does the multimedia structure of educational resources relate to their content, pedagogy, and institutional context?}

Understanding this relationship matters for several reasons.
First, multimedia resource availability varies dramatically across institutions and disciplines---a computer science course at MIT may include 40 hours of lecture video, while an equivalent course at another university provides only a syllabus.
Second, the presence or absence of multimedia components reflects pedagogical choices that are not captured in text descriptions alone.
Third, cross-modal analysis---predicting what kinds of multimedia resources a course offers from its textual description---can inform resource allocation, curriculum planning, and open educational resource (OER) development~\cite{aucancela2023ersslr}.

We address this gap with \ADSMM, a multimodal extension of the Agent-Didactic Spaces educational benchmark.
Our contributions:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Multimodal educational resource dataset.}
  We augment a cross-national educational benchmark ({\NumCourses} courses, {\NumUniversities} universities, {\NumCountries} countries) with structured multimodal resource annotations from MIT OpenCourseWare: {\NumResourceTypes} resource types across {\NumOCWFetched} courses, with {\NumOCWMatched} matched to the ADS text corpus.

  \item \textbf{Cross-modal evaluation tasks.}
  We define two evaluated tasks---multimedia richness prediction from text and resource type classification---and specify a third open task (cross-institutional transfer) enabled by the cross-national structure.

  \item \textbf{Latent space analysis.}
  We show that courses, occupations, and missions from {\NumUniversities} universities form a \emph{unified didactic space}: institutions overlap rather than cluster separately, and the primary axis of this space correlates with multimedia resource richness ($r{=}0.41$).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What makes this a multimedia dataset?}
Unlike MOOC platforms that host video content directly, university course catalogs describe courses as text but \emph{link to} multimedia resources hosted elsewhere.
\ADSMM bridges this gap by collecting structured multimodal annotations for each course---not the raw media files, but a standardized description of what media types exist, enabling large-scale cross-modal analysis without requiring petabytes of video storage.
This metadata-centric approach enables research on \emph{multimedia resource structure} rather than \emph{multimedia content analysis}, complementing content-focused datasets such as MultiHateClip~\cite{multihateclip2024} at ACM~MM.


\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Educational datasets.}
Existing educational benchmarks fall into several categories.
\emph{Learner interaction} datasets (KDD Cup 2010~\cite{stamper2010kddcup}, EdNet~\cite{choi2020ednet}, OULAD~\cite{kuzilek2017oulad}) track student behavior at single institutions; they measure what learners \emph{do} but not what resources are \emph{available}.
\emph{MOOC catalog} datasets aggregate course metadata: MOOCCubeX~\cite{yu2023mooccubex} provides 230K video subtitles and 358K exercises from XuetangX---the largest multimodal educational dataset---but covers a single Chinese platform without cross-national scope or labor-market integration. The MOOC literature more broadly has grown rapidly~\cite{zawackirichter2019mooc}, yet large-scale cross-institutional resource metadata remains scarce.
\emph{Skill mapping} datasets like the Course-Skill Atlas~\cite{courseskillatlas2024} (3M syllabi, 3K US institutions) link curricula to O*NET skills at scale, but contain no multimedia annotations; related work on skill extraction~\cite{zhang2022skillspan,decorte2023skillextraction} and labor-market forecasting~\cite{wef2023futureofwork,shi2020} focuses on job postings rather than educational resources.
\emph{Lecture-level} datasets like M3AV~\cite{m3av2024} (367 hours, audio+video+slides) and Lecture Presentations~\cite{lee2023lecture} provide dense multimodal annotations for individual lectures but do not address cross-institutional curriculum structure.
\ADSMM occupies a distinct niche: cross-national course-level resource structure with multimedia annotations and labor-market data.

\paragraph{Multimodal learning.}
Mayer's cognitive theory of multimedia learning~\cite{mayer2009multimedia,mayer2014multimedia} establishes that combining text with visual and auditory channels improves learning outcomes---but research on \emph{which} courses offer \emph{which} media types at institutional scale is limited.
Multimodal learning analytics~\cite{sharma2020mmla_survey,ifenthaler2020learning} use sensor data (gaze, gesture, speech) to study learner behavior, not resource structure.
Video lecture analysis~\cite{bhat2023lecturevideosurvey} processes existing video content but does not address the availability or distribution of video resources across curricula.
Recent multimodal foundation models~\cite{xu2023mplug2,bommasani2022foundation} enable cross-modal understanding, but lack educational benchmarks to evaluate their application in curriculum analysis.

\paragraph{Cross-modal retrieval and representation.}
CLIP~\cite{radford2021clip} and its descendants~\cite{luo2022clip4clip,clip2tf2024} enable cross-modal retrieval (text$\leftrightarrow$video), while sentence embeddings~\cite{reimers2019sentencebert,wang2020minilm,devlin2019bert} provide text-only representations.
\ADSMM complements within-resource retrieval by providing a dataset-level view: \emph{which courses have video at all}, enabling analysis of multimedia resource distribution rather than within-resource search.

\paragraph{Open educational resources.}
MIT OCW~\cite{mitocw2001} pioneered open course materials; UNESCO~\cite{unesco2019oer} and OER researchers~\cite{wiley2014oerdefinition,tlili2023oerreview} analyze policy and adoption but lack structured, machine-readable resource type annotations across institutions.
Dataset documentation practices~\cite{gebru2021datasheets,bender2018datasheets} inform our release, which follows the Datasheets for Datasets template.

\paragraph{Multi-stakeholder evaluation and cross-national education.}
Multi-stakeholder recommendation~\cite{abdollahpouri2020multistakeholder,burke2017multistakeholder} argues that evaluation quality cannot be assessed from a single perspective; fairness in educational recommendation~\cite{ekstrand2019fairness,baker2019challenges} adds urgency to this concern.
Cross-national curriculum comparison~\cite{oecd2023education,marginson2022global} is well-studied in policy research but lacks computational tools at scale.
ACM MM dataset papers~\cite{multihateclip2024,synopground2024,dfbench2025,emoart2025} typically focus on content analysis; \ADSMM extends the scope to educational resource structure.

\section{The ADS-MM Dataset}
\label{sec:dataset}

\ADSMM comprises two layers: a \emph{text layer} of cross-national educational artifacts and a \emph{multimodal annotation layer} with structured resource metadata.

\subsection{Text Layer: Cross-National Educational Artifacts}
\label{subsec:text-layer}

The text layer contains {\NumArtifacts} primary text artifacts from {\NumUniversities} universities across {\NumCountries} countries:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Courses} ({\NumCourses}): course descriptions from MIT OCW ({\NumMIT}), UC Berkeley ({\NumUCB}), Stanford ({\NumStanford}), UIUC ({\NumUIUC}), Cornell ({\NumCornell}), KTH Sweden ({\NumKTH}), University of Edinburgh ({\NumEdinburgh}), and MISIS Russia ({\NumMISIS}).
  \item \textbf{O*NET occupations} ({\NumOccupations}): occupation profiles with required skills, tasks, and technologies from the US Department of Labor~\cite{onet2025,giabelli2021skills2job}.
  \item \textbf{Mission statements} ({\NumMissions}): university mission and vision texts.
\end{itemize}

All sources are publicly available with full provenance tracking (source URLs, content hashes, collection timestamps).

\subsection{Multimodal Annotation Layer: OCW Resource Metadata}
\label{subsec:multimodal}

For the MIT OCW subset, we collect structured multimodal resource annotations by querying the official OCW course metadata (JSON endpoints) for each of {\NumOCWTotal} courses listed in the MIT OCW sitemap.
Each course record includes:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Resource type flags}: binary indicators for video lectures, lecture notes/slides, interactive simulations, and assessments (problem sets, exams).
  \item \textbf{Resource richness score} (0--6): count of distinct resource categories present (video, audio, notes, visual, interactive, assessment).
  \item \textbf{Topic hierarchy}: multi-level subject classification from OCW's taxonomy.
  \item \textbf{Course images}: cover images for all {\NumImages} courses (mean 82~KB, total 134~MB), providing a visual modality.
  \item \textbf{Lecture content} (video courses): for {\NumOCWVideo} video courses, we extract structured lecture titles (median 26 per course), syllabi, and calendar text (median 13,679 characters), enriching text representations beyond catalog descriptions.
  \item \textbf{Temporal metadata}: semester, year, and course level.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Collection process.}
We parse the MIT OCW sitemap (\texttt{sitemap.xml}) to extract all course slugs, then fetch structured metadata (\texttt{data.json}) for each course.
Course numbers are extracted from URL slugs and matched to ADS catalog entries via normalized identifiers.
The collection script runs in approximately 15 minutes with a 0.3-second polite delay between requests.
All data is publicly available under MIT OCW's CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license.

\paragraph{Multimodal resource statistics.}
Of {\NumOCWFetched} OCW courses fetched, {\NumOCWMultimedia} ({\OCWMultimediaPercent}) include at least one multimedia resource type: {\NumOCWVideo} with video lectures, {\NumOCWAudio} with audio content, and {\NumOCWVisual} with visual galleries or instructor insights.
All {\NumOCWFetched} courses include a cover image.
We identify {\NumResourceTypes} distinct resource type values across the corpus, grouped into 6 categories: video, audio, notes, visual, interactive, and assessment.
The mean resource richness is {\MeanRichness}$\pm${\StdRichness} (scale 0--6).
Of these, {\NumOCWMatched} ({\OCWMatchRate}) match courses in the ADS text corpus, enabling cross-modal analysis.


\subsection{Cross-National Context}
\label{subsec:cross-national}

A key feature of \ADSMM is the cross-national dimension spanning {\NumCountries} countries with complementary modalities.
MIT OCW provides multimedia resource annotations; MISIS (Russia) contributes a distinct \emph{instructional activity modality}: each of {\NumMISIS} courses includes structured hours across lectures (83\%), practical sessions (91\%), and laboratory work (22\%), plus {\MISISCompetencies} FGOS competency codes and a prerequisite graph with {\MISISPrereqLinks} links across {\MISISPrograms} programs over {\MISISYears} years.
This creates a testbed for \emph{cross-institutional transfer}: can models trained on MIT's multimedia structure predict resource availability at universities with different educational traditions?
The five US universities share a curricular tradition; KTH (Sweden), Edinburgh (UK), and MISIS (Russia) provide out-of-distribution test cases.


\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We define two cross-modal evaluation tasks and evaluate baselines on each.
\emph{Task~1 (Richness Prediction)}: given a course text description, predict the resource richness score (0--6); evaluated with MAE and Spearman~$\rho$.
\emph{Task~2 (Resource Type Classification)}: predict which resource types a course provides (video, notes, assessment) as a multi-label classification problem; evaluated with per-type and macro~$F_1$.
The cross-national structure also enables a transfer task (train on MIT, predict other universities), which we analyze qualitatively via latent space geometry~\cite{shi2024mllmedu} in Section~\ref{subsec:latent}.

Multimedia resource availability varies substantially across departments (Figure~\ref{fig:resource-dist}).
EECS-related departments (6, 18) have the highest video and notes density, while humanities and social science departments rely more heavily on text-based materials.
The overall richness distribution (Figure~\ref{fig:richness-hist}) shows a mean of {\MeanRichness}$\pm${\StdRichness}, with the majority of courses at richness 1--2 (primarily text + assessment).

\subsection{Cross-Modal Baselines}

SBERT embeddings achieve $\rho{=}{\SBERTRichnessRho}$ on richness prediction and macro $F_1{=}{\SBERTMacroF}$ on resource type classification (Table~\ref{tab:baselines}), a 47\% improvement in $\rho$ over the department prior and 2.9$\times$ improvement in $F_1$ over unbalanced baselines.

This demonstrates that course text descriptions encode implicit signals about multimedia instructional design---even without access to the actual media content.

\subsection{Latent Space Analysis}
\label{subsec:latent}

We embed all 36{,}192 canonical records with Sentence-BERT~\cite{reimers2019sentencebert} (all-MiniLM-L6-v2, 384d) and analyze whether the dataset supports a \emph{unified didactic space}---a shared embedding where courses, occupations, and missions from heterogeneous institutions coexist.

The separation ratio (mean inter-institution distance / mean intra-institution distance) is 0.70, well below the threshold of 2.0, confirming that institutions \emph{overlap} rather than form isolated clusters. The closest pair is MIT--Stanford (0.20); the most distant is MISIS--ASU (0.94), reflecting language and curricular tradition differences. For 200 randomly sampled MIT courses, 36.4\% of nearest neighbors ($k{=}10$) come from other institutions---UC Berkeley (21.5\%), KTH (5.5\%), Stanford (5.3\%)---demonstrating that semantic similarity crosses institutional and national boundaries.

Most strikingly, PCA on the MIT OCW subset reveals that the first principal component correlates with multimedia resource richness ($r{=}0.41$, $p{<}0.001$; Figure~\ref{fig:pc-richness}), and a second independent axis (PC3) also correlates ($r{=}{-}0.23$). Courses with video are displaced from non-video courses (centroid shift\,=\,0.17). The embedding space---learned from text alone, using contrastive pre-training~\cite{chen2020simclr,li2024multimodaledu} principles---captures multimedia structure as a geometric property. Figure~\ref{fig:latent-pca} shows the full PCA projection colored by institution.

MISIS provides complementary evidence: courses with laboratory work occupy a different latent region than lecture-only courses (shift\,=\,0.09), and MISIS forms the tightest cluster (intra-distance 0.54 vs.\ MIT's 0.90), consistent with centralized FGOS standards producing more homogeneous curricula than decentralized US departments. The course--occupation centroid distance is 0.60, while the course--mission distance is only 0.40, suggesting that universities successfully encode their institutional missions into curriculum content.


\section{Limitations, Ethics, and Availability}
\label{sec:limitations}

\ADSMM's multimodal annotations cover MIT OCW only; extending to other institutions requires equivalent open metadata, which most universities do not provide.
Resource type annotations capture \emph{availability}, not content quality or usage---a course marked as having video may have 2 or 40 lectures.
The dataset spans {\NumCountries} countries but remains US-heavy (73\% of courses); O*NET~\cite{onet2025} reflects US labor market structure~\cite{wef2023futureofwork}; MISIS includes Russian-language descriptions alongside English competency codes.

All data is from public sources under open licenses (MIT OCW: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; O*NET: public domain). No personally identifiable information is included.
The complete dataset, collection scripts, and evaluation code are available at \url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19363580}, with annual updates planned as OCW releases new content.


\section{Conclusion}

We presented \ADSMM, a cross-national educational resource dataset with multimodal annotations spanning {\NumCourses} courses from {\NumUniversities} universities in {\NumCountries} countries.
Three findings emerge.
First, course text descriptions encode multimedia instructional design: SBERT embeddings predict video availability ($F_1{=}{\SBERTVideoF}$) and resource richness ($\rho{=}{\SBERTRichnessRho}$).
Second, the primary axis of the educational embedding space correlates with multimedia richness ($r{=}0.41$)---text-only embeddings capture modality as a geometric property.
Third, courses from 8 universities form a \emph{unified didactic space} (separation ratio 0.70) where 36\% of nearest neighbors cross institutional boundaries, suggesting that curricular content transcends institutional and national context~\cite{marginson2022global}.
We release all data, images, code, and baselines to enable research at the intersection of multimedia and education.
